About

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Computer Science at Cornell University, advised by Carla P. Gomes. I also work closely with Christopher De Sa and Volodymyr Kuleshov. I am affiliated with Cornell AI for Science Institute and AI-LEAF Institute. My research studies knowledge-centric AI with an emphasis on scientific reasoning. I was also a student researcher at Amazon Grand Challenge, Amazon AWS AI Research & Education, Microsoft Research Asia, and NEC Laboratories America, where my research led to impactful papers and patents on computational antibody design, long-context protein language models, time-series representation learning, and expressive graph neural networks. My research has been generously supported by the Cornell Presidential Life Science Fellowship and the Google AI Academic Program.

I am currently on the job market — please feel free to reach out!

Research Interests

My research develops knowledge-centric AI for scientific discovery: AI that uses scientific knowledge to shape how it predicts, learns, and reasons, especially when data are limited and predictions must go beyond observed conditions. I pursue this through three complementary approaches (scientific constraints, representations, and feedback) and help build shared infrastructure for AI and science, in close collaboration with physicists, chemists, and biologists:

  • Scientific constraints (building science into reasoning): physics-constrained spectrum prediction ICML26 Science Advances AAAI24 NeurIPS22 AI4Science, constrained search & probabilistic inference for molecular identification AAAI26 ICLR27 Nature, generative inverse imaging ICCV25 CV4MS
  • Scientific representations (turning knowledge into capability): representation alignment & disentanglement in generative models ICLR27 ICML23, expressive variational inference AAAI25, long-context protein language models ICLR26, self-supervised molecular & drug-interaction learning WWW21, latent reasoning in LLMs ICLR27, multi-modal materials representations ICLR25 AI4Mat
  • Scientific feedback (from limited evidence to discovery): feedback-efficient adaptation of generative models ICLR27, reinforcement learning for honest reasoning TMLR, continual learning for agents NeurIPS26 TTCL, LLM agents with execution feedback ICLR26
  • Shared infrastructure (benchmarks & tools for AI and science): materials discovery benchmarks NeurIPS23, agentic optimization benchmarks ICLR26, evaluating scientific reasoning of LLMs Nature ICML26, 2-bit LLM fine-tuning on consumer GPUs ICLR25, generative molecular design review Nature Machine Intelligence
  • Scientific domains: materials science (spectra & materials screening), chemistry (molecular identification), biology (protein design & genomics), and medicine
