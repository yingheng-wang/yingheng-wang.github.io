About

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Computer Science at Cornell University with Carla P. Gomes. I also work closely with Volodymyr Kuleshov and Christopher De Sa. I am affiliated with Cornell AI for Science Institute and AI-LEAF Institute. My research studies knowledge-centric AI with an emphasis on scientific reasoning. I was also student researcher at Amazon Grand Challenge, AWS AI Lab, Microsoft Research, and NEC Lab America, where my research led to impactful papers and patents on computational antibody design, protein language models, time-series foundation models, and efficient neural architectures. My research has been generously supported by Cornell Presidential Life Science Fellowship.

I am currently on the job market and actively seeking faculty and research scientist positions — please feel free to reach out!

Research Interests

My research centers on developing knowledge-centric AI methods for scientific reasoning, which integrate physical and domain knowledge into modern AI techniques to improve their efficacy, efficiency, robustness, and interpretability. Current research topics include:

  • Learning with physical knowledge: constrained learning ICML26, symmetry learning AAAI24, disentangled representations ICML23
  • Learning under limited resources: self-supervised & unsupervised learning AAAI23 TheWebConf, multi-modal learning ICLR25 AI4Mat expressive and efficient neural networks ICLR25 NeurIPS23 ICML22
  • Reasoning & inference with learning: reinforcement learning ICLR26 ICLR26, constrained reasoning ICLR26 AAAI26 AAAI26, probabilistic inference AAAI25
  • Generative AI: large language models ICLR25 ICML26, diffusion & flow-based models ICML23 AAAI25 ICCV25 CV4MS, generative agents ICLR26
  • Applications: scientific discovery (biology, materials, medicine, and sustainability)
  • Taxonomy & Datasets & Benchmarks: genrative molecule design Nature Machine Intelligence, machine learning for materials discovery NeurIPS23, structured data augmentation IEEE Data Engineering Bulletin, expert-level combinatorial reasoning of LLMs ICLR26, expert-level scientific reasoning of LLMs Nature
These thrusts converge to build AI systems that can reason, learn efficiently, and generalize robustly in complex real-world scenarios, ultimately advancing scientific discovery and innovation.
